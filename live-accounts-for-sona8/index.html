Positioning

Why a plan built from leaders alone is incomplete, and where Sona8 fits.

The passages cited below describe who leads or who transfers: founders and a president who stay, a general manager, a CEO, about 250 people carrying over. They do not describe what the wider acquired team would say about how work really runs, which is the part Sona8's interviews are built to collect.

The Commodity Trap

Leading with "AI interviews"

Leading with the technology invites a comparison with survey tools and asks the buyer to judge a feature, when the decision in front of them is about a deal that has just closed.

The High-Converting Angle

Hearing the whole acquired team while the plan is still being written

Sona8 states its own method: everyone gets one main question, answered by voice or text in their own language, and a theme counts when six of ten coding agents agree. Leaders see how many people raised each point and the words they used, nobody is rated, and the same question can be asked again after action is taken.

Source: sona8.com, Sona8's own description of its method. These are statements about how it works, not measured results, and not a prediction for any account below.

Limits stated up front

What this list does not claim

  • 4 of 9 accounts have a second source that is not the acquirer. Inseego, EVI Industries, Axogen, Climb Global Solutions each pair the acquirer's own filing or release with a publisher it does not control: a seller newsroom, a target's own site, or trade press. The other 5 (National CineMedia, Unusual Machines, HF Foods Group, Data I/O, Rank One Computing) rest on two documents from the acquirer's own filings, the 8-K and its press release. Both describe the same deal, so they confirm that it closed and what the acquirer says about its people; they are not independent confirmation. The Evidence column marks which is which.
  • A closing date is not a problem. Nothing here says any account has an integration problem. Each row shows a deal that closed in the last 60 days and the passages in which the acquirer, or someone else, describes the people involved.
  • The list covers listed companies only. Acquirers were found through SEC filings, which only public companies file. Private buyers, and most private equity add-ons, announce a deal by press release alone and are not here.
  • Headcount and market value come from each acquirer's latest 10-K. Employee counts are from the 10-K (Rank One Computing from its first 10-K, covering 82 people including 16 part-time). Market values are the non-affiliate figure printed on the 10-K cover, so they carry the date on that cover, in some cases mid-2025. Rank One Computing listed in February 2026 and has no such figure, so its value is a 4 September 2026 quote of $4.31 times the 19,080,127 shares on its 10-K cover.
  • People counts are the companies' own statements. The figure of about 250 people carrying over to Inseego appears in Inseego's release, on Nokia's newsroom and in SDxCentral's report. All three give the same figure and none is an independent head count.
  • Personas are role titles. No named individual was verified for any account; confirm the right person before outreach.

Patterns

Three ways a closed deal shows its people are still moving.

An account enters this dispatch only when two public documents agree that a deal closed in the last 60 days and say something about the people involved. We keep a dated copy of each and confirm the quoted text is physically in it before drafting a single word. 4 accounts have a second source outside the acquirer; 5 rest on the acquirer's own filing and release, and the table says which.

PATTERN 01

Closed Across A Border

The acquired business or its people sit in a different country from the buyer: Inseego, Climb Global Solutions, HF Foods Group. Teams on both sides work in different places and, often, different languages.

Verifiable Signals
  • Completion reported in an SEC 8-K filed in the last 60 days
  • The acquired business or its hubs named in another country in the release or in coverage
Sona8 angle: interviews run in each person's own language, so people on both sides of a border are heard in the same set of themes.
PATTERN 02

Closed, With The Acquired Team Staying On

The release names who continues to lead, or says people are being hired or are already integrated: EVI Industries, Unusual Machines, Rank One Computing.

Verifiable Signals
  • Completion reported in an SEC 8-K filed in the last 60 days
  • A release or article naming the leaders or team that stay, or describing hiring or integration at the acquired site
Sona8 angle: when the team stays on, a voice interview with each person captures how work runs today, in their words, before the plan changes it.
PATTERN 03

Closed, With A New Combined Platform

The deal creates a combined platform, division or product line that the two organisations now run together: Axogen, National CineMedia, Data I/O.

Verifiable Signals
  • Completion reported in an SEC 8-K filed in the last 60 days
  • A release or the target's own site describing the combined platform or division
Sona8 angle: a combined platform is run by people who until recently worked in two organisations; interviews with everyone show where their ways of working meet.
