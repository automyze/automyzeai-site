05 · Veriad's edge

What is real today, and what still has to be shown.

Veriad has a sharply defined buyer, founders with relevant backgrounds and a market with dated tailwinds. It does not yet have named customers, a stated mechanism or public proof of results. This page separates the three.

The edge in one paragraph

A sharp buyer, a calendar, and a team.

Veriad's edge today is positional, not yet technical. It has a precisely named ideal customer (consumer-facing, regulated, $50M+ ad spend, a review team with bottlenecks), a wedge whose triggers regulators publish on a schedule, and two founders whose backgrounds in compliance software and enterprise sales match the product. Verified What it has not yet shown is how the agent works, what it has delivered for anyone, or why the platforms' own automation will not absorb the use case. Verified

What the edge is not yet

Three things to avoid claiming on its behalf.

  • Not yet a data moat. Precedent ingestion becomes a moat only when customers' accumulated decisions improve results in ways a new entrant cannot copy. That has not been shown.
  • Not yet a platform-proof position. If Meta and Google fold compliance and brand-safety checks into their own tools, a neutral layer needs a reason to exist beyond neutrality.
  • Not yet a customer story. Every "customers like you" line would be fabricated today. Use design-partner outcomes once they exist, never before.

What would turn the edge into proof

In priority order.

  1. A design-partner programme. Three to five regulated brands, each offered a free retrospective review of their last 90 days of public ads. It produces the first referenceable outcomes and the first real precedent data.
  2. A public benchmark from open data. For example an analysis of published ad rulings by vertical. It needs no customer and shows the team's depth in the exact domain.
  3. A clear statement of the "Backed by" row. If these are investors, say so accurately; if customers, name the relationship.
  4. A direct answer on platform-native automation. One page that says what stays under the brand's control when the platforms automate.
  5. Security milestones as launches. Each of SOC 2 and ISO 27001 is a dated event that unblocks stalled enterprise deals.
  6. One positioning decision. Lead with compliance and present adOS as the expansion path, or the reverse, but choose.

Where we would lead

Our read.

Lead with the wedge. It has dated, verifiable pain events; its buyer is named in Veriad's own launch post; and the design-partner motion can run on the segments in this report. Use the vision's signals as account qualifiers, so that an account which is in-housing or rebuilding measurement is flagged as a future expansion, not a day-one pitch. Hypothesis

What would change our view. A named customer with a measurable outcome, a public explanation of how precedent data compounds, or evidence that the platforms' native tools cannot do cross-channel creative compliance would each move a rating from unproven to plausible.

Questions worth asking

Before anyone commits time.

  1. What is live today: one agent or eight modules?
  2. Who are the customers, and what does the "Backed by" row mean?
  3. How do Meta's and Google's own automation roadmaps change the pitch?
  4. Who does founder-led sales while the product is still being built?
  5. Which market is first: UK, EU or US?
